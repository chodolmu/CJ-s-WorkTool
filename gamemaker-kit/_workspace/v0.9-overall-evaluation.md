# gamemaker-kit v0.9.0 — Overall Evaluation (independent, holistic)

**Evaluator**: external, asked only to judge product maturity vs v1.0 readiness.
**Date**: 2026-05-18
**Scope**: actual code/docs/templates/scripts in the repo at HEAD `fcb5f0f`. Self-evaluations (`v0.9-protocol-*.md`, HANDOFF, CHANGELOG narratives) were NOT used as evidence — only as claims to verify against the artifacts.

---

## §1 Verdict

**v0.9 is materially v1.0-worthy, and the v0.5–v0.9 evaluator-cycle work has tipped from net-positive into the early stages of net-negative — perfectionism via meta-tooling.** The functional surface (29 SKILLs, 4 agents, 4 templates, bot hook lib, 6-stage port gate, pre-flight script) is coherent, internally consistent, and concretely executable; the schemas are stable and additive-only; the SKILL contracts cross-reference correctly; the structural guards mechanically catch the defect classes they target. What's blocking v1.0 is not a missing capability — it is the absence of a single piece of real-user external evidence that the kit works end-to-end on a non-author project. The honest answer to *"why hasn't this been bumped to v1.0?"* is **"because no one has run it on a fresh game yet."** That's a legitimate v1.0 blocker — but it is the only one, and Protocols 1/3/4 cannot produce it.

## §2 What the kit does well

### 2.1 The runtime backbone is genuine, not handwaved

- `templates/_bot_hook_lib.js:14-39` is a real LCG RNG (Numerical Recipes constants, `>>>0` masking, degenerate-seed handling) — not a placeholder. `makeHook` at L85-240 does actual crash containment, action counting, sim-time bounding, and degrades each optional persona callback to `null` on exception (L197-229). Same-seed determinism is enforceable by the 5-point smoke (`gmk-prototype-rules:151-156`) and `gmk-validate:39` actually wires it (two consecutive seed=0 runs, deep-equal summary).
- `templates/prototype-grid.html:14-41`/`55-120` is a working merge-grid game, not a `<!-- TODO -->` shell. The four prototype templates total ~1,000 lines of real game scaffolding bound to the hook contract.
- `skills/gmk-validate/SKILL.md:97-180` ships the actual `chooseAction`-bearing Playwright runner inline as the skill output, with per-persona policy switching, prune-at-30, and crash-tolerant trial recording.

### 2.2 The structural refusal architecture is real and grep-able

- `skills/gmk-port/SKILL.md:24-28` enforces *both* `validation.verdict === "PASS"` AND `self_test.latest_verdict === "PASS"` — not either — and stamps `forced: true` if `--force` is used, which is the kind of audit trail that survives misuse.
- `scripts/check-plugin-meta.sh` runs cleanly against HEAD (verified: all 7 checks green). Check A actually iterates `Run /gmk-` hit lines per-SKILL and consults a per-line allowlist (L107-137) — that is harder than a file-level grep, and it is what makes Rule 14 falsifiable.
- All 27 SKILLs with `## Preconditions` carry the `Rule 13-14, 16` footer (verified via the script). The 2 that don't (`gmk-init`, `gmk-prototype-rules`) are correctly out of scope — init creates state, the rulebook reads no state.

### 2.3 Agent integration is broader than the May 14 audit said

The internal audit noted *"only 2 SKILLs reference agents by name"*. As of v0.9, 9 SKILLs reference agents (28 total `@agent-name` occurrences) and the references are operational, not decorative — e.g., `gmk-port:81-92` is gated on `systems-designer` for non-trivial mechanics with a 4-row routing table. `playtest-analyst.md:10-12,17-25` is the one-way-valve as designed (log-reader-of-last-resort, max-iteration=1, refuses "fix it").

### 2.4 Internal consistency held across 9 versions

- The 17 rules in `gmk-prototype-rules` are non-overlapping and ordered by abstraction (1-7 mechanical, 8-12 deterministic-behavior, 13-15 contract-shape, 16-17 schema read contracts). No rule contradicts another. The recurring "warn, never refuse" pattern (Rules 16/17) honors the v0.4 "no data loss" promise without exception.
- The `kit_version` chain — written by `gmk-init` (`pillars-example.json:3`), verified by Check D, read per Rule 16 — is fully closed. The Rule 16 §"Exercise paths" subsection at `prototype-rules:483-496` honestly distinguishes the live row from the safety-valve rows; that is the right call (synthetic fixtures for unreachable rows are anti-patterns).
- Refuse-chain Rule 14 cycle-prevention has an actual case-list (`prototype-rules:394-396` enumerates shader-INCONCLUSIVE and validate--skip as already-handled), and the CYCLE form is correctly preserved as a safety valve with the same policy that worked for Rule 16 rows 1/3/4.

### 2.5 The "what we will not do" list is structural, not cosmetic

`README.md:139-147` and `CONCEPT.md:343-348` name 5 categories of refuse-forever scope (release/live-ops, external testers, 3D/MMO/PvP, vibe-coding market, two-way sync). This is the rare kit that documents the bounded shape *and then sticks to it across 9 versions*. The bot-vs-fun distinction (`CONCEPT.md:424-451`) is academically honest — fun stays the user's judgment, the kit's word is "falsification".

### 2.6 The v1.0 backcompat inventory is the right artifact at the right time

`_workspace/v1.0-backcompat-inventory.md` enumerates 6 categories of v0.x guarantees with file:line provenance and explicit break-candidate menus. The closing recommendation (Categories 1, 4, 6 worth breaking; 2, 3, 5 not) is defensible. This is the document a v1.0 work-start needs.

## §3 What the kit doesn't do well

### 3.1 Zero external dogfood evidence

The kit has been built and refined by the same author who specified it. There is no record in the repo of a non-author user running `/gmk-init` → `/gmk-roadmap` → `/gmk-prototype` → `/gmk-validate` → `/gmk-self-test` → `/gmk-port` end-to-end. The CHANGELOG's "ACCURATE" verdicts are about *protocol compliance*, not about *real users completing the workflow*. The kit's own README §27-39 prescribes the first-week workflow but no first-week trace exists. **This is the single biggest gap between v0.9 and v1.0.**

### 3.2 The onboarding cliff is steep

A new user must internalize, before being productive:
- 4 axes (time / discipline / validation / integration)
- 17 rules (with Rule 14 having two forms, Rule 16 having 4 rows, Rule 17 having 4 rows)
- 29 SKILLs (some with overlapping triggers — `gmk-roadmap` vs `gmk-task-split`, `gmk-platform-check` vs `gmk-portability-check`)
- 4 agents with strict precondition tables
- A 5-point hook smoke, a 4-shape advisor, a 6-stage port gate, a 4-persona bot policy
- The asymmetric self-test verdict rule (`gmk-self-test:167`)
- 3 deferred upgrade triggers (re-roadmap / kill --revive / accept-regression)

There is no `QUICKSTART.md`. `README.md:27-39` is the closest thing but it assumes the user already gets the conceptual model. A 10-minute *"here's a 3-pillar zoo game from `gmk-init` to a `validate` PASS"* walkthrough — even fictional — would materially flatten the cliff.

### 3.3 `gmk-port` is 684 lines and *currently the only non-template artifact over the doc-cap*

The kit explicitly enforces 300/600-line caps on prototypes (Rule 2) but lets `gmk-port:1-684` exceed both. The v0.9 "keep it" decision (`v0.9-gmk-port-split-decision.md`) is reasoned but the 5 re-open triggers it lists are all *future* conditions; none of them grade the current cognitive-load cost the cap was designed to prevent. The cap's own rationale (`prototype-rules:58` — "mental-model load exceeds what a single reader can hold") applies to SKILL.md prose too, not only to mechanic JS. Either extend Rule 2 to SKILLs with a clear carve-out, or split `gmk-port` per stage now — but leaving the single largest SKILL above the user-facing cap is a credibility tension.

### 3.4 Some SKILLs feel narrow-scenario rather than core path

- `gmk-narrative.md` (234 lines) — explicitly optional, only fires for `shape='dialogue'` milestones, and the bot validator is INCONCLUSIVE-by-design for that shape (`gmk-validate:68`). Practical reachability is low.
- `gmk-save-migrate.md` (234 lines) — fires only after port, for milestones that change persisted save schema. Unclear how many milestones in a 2D/≤5min/deterministic game actually touch persistence.
- `gmk-mechanic-merge.md` (257 lines) — requires 2 prior PASS milestones with combinable mechanics. Real but late-game.
- `gmk-art-gen.md` — wraps an external `/art` skill (`CLAUDE.md`) the kit doesn't ship and the user must run separately on a ComfyUI server. The wrap is honest but the dependency is heavy.

Calling these *ornaments* would be too strong — they are sketched honestly, with refusals and preconditions. Calling them *core path* would be wrong. The 4-axis coverage is real on time/validation/integration but discipline-axis density is uneven (design/code/qa are dense; narrative/save are thin).

### 3.5 The HANDOFF / CHANGELOG / Protocol triptych is now its own thing

`HANDOFF.md` is 778 lines. `CHANGELOG.md` is 604 lines. The v0.9 release alone produced 5 new `_workspace/v0.9-*.md` files. The author's process discipline is producing a parallel corpus of *meta-artifacts about the kit's quality*, and that corpus is growing faster than the kit's user-facing surface. The 4-consecutive-ACCURATE chain is real, but the cost-side is that v0.6–v0.9 added ~3 new SKILL features total (Rule 16, Rule 17, Checks D/E/F/G, pre-push hook) at the cost of ~2,000 lines of evaluator-cycle prose. The ratio is tilting.

### 3.6 Minor surface drift

- `README.md:154-173` has the `_workspace/` tree and `CONCEPT.md:76-105` has a different one (CONCEPT lists `system-spec.md` and `feel-numbers.md` outputs that README doesn't). Neither is wrong; they're at different abstraction levels. New users will see a difference and wonder.
- `README.md:181-194` comparison table is labeled "gamemaker-kit v0.2" not v0.9. Stale.
- `CONCEPT.md:332-340` v0.3/v0.4 phased plans (visual diff, GVGAI bots, LangGraph upgrade) are still listed as "planned" but the kit shipped past v0.4 without doing them. v0.9 reframed dev-complete as a checkpoint; that section needs a parallel reframe.

## §4 Real vs theoretical defects in v0.5–v0.9 evaluator cycles

The user asked me to adjudicate which of the v0.5–v0.9 evaluator-caught issues were *real and shipped-blocking* vs *theoretical / anchoring-effect / process-justifying*. Reading CHANGELOG against the artifacts:

### Real and shipped-blocking (would have caused user-visible defects)

| Cycle | Defect | Evidence it was real |
|---|---|---|
| v0.5 | 7 v0.4 regressions: deprecated fields written by stale skills, partial sweeps | `CHANGELOG.md:250-307` documents specific fields (`kill_history[]`, `validation_history[]`, `coded_themes`) that were declared deprecated but still written. A user opening v0.4 milestones.json would have gotten polluted files. **Real.** |
| v0.6 | Rule 14 token half-applied (`gmk-prototype-rules:398` declared in v0.5; only 2 SKILLs applied) | Refuse-chain cycles would silently dead-end users (`prototype-rules:354-362`). Check A now mechanically enforces 29-SKILL coverage. **Real.** |
| v0.7 | `kit_version` write-only since v0.4 (no SKILL read it) | Future kit downgrades would have silently consumed newer files without warning. Rule 16 + Check D close this. **Real but defensive** — no user had hit it, but the kit ships as a versioned plugin so the gap was structural. |
| v0.8 | Check B promotion from WARN→FAIL caught a stale `marketplace.json:8` allowlist entry that *never matched* | `CHANGELOG.md:128-130` — the allowlisted line never contained "endpoint"; M-1 audit removed it. **Real bug in the guard itself**, not in the kit's user-facing behavior. Self-validating: the kit's own audit found its own audit bug. |
| v0.8 | `pillars.kind` field declared since v0.4, never read until Rule 17 | Same shape as Rule 14 and Rule 16. **Real but defensive.** Two SKILLs (`gmk-prototype`, `gmk-self-test`) now route on it; 25 don't (correctly). |
| v0.9 | `_save_breaking` field — 1 mention, 0 readers, 0 user adoption | Dropping it (Path b) was correct. **Real cleanup**, not a defect. |

### Theoretical / anchoring (would not have produced user-visible failures)

| Cycle | Defect | Why it was theoretical |
|---|---|---|
| v0.9 | M-2 original framing — "4 dead branches" (Rule 14 CYCLE, `kit_version > current`, `early_fail`, `Math.random`) | Protocol 1 itself caught that 3 of 4 were live (`CHANGELOG.md:11`, `prototype-rules:496`). Only the 3 `kit_version` rows actually qualified, and the right answer was annotation, not synthetic fixtures. The candidate as originally written was anchoring on a pattern (Rule 14-shape sweeps) and over-applying it. |
| v0.9 | Rule 16 §Exercise-paths annotation itself | Closes a meta-gap (declared rule, unexercised rows) that no user would ever notice. The annotation is intellectually honest but ships no behavior change. The cost was 14 lines of documentation; the benefit is process-internal. |
| v0.8/v0.9 | F21 anchoring-effect tracking, correction ratio per release (1.75 → 2.80 → 3.00) | The ratio is rising because the protocol template gets stricter, not because authorship gets worse. The metric is increasingly measuring its own evolution. Closure criterion (ratio < 1.0 for 3 consecutive releases) is plausibly never achievable as long as Protocol 1 itself keeps tightening. |
| v0.9 | Skill count `~28 → 29` across 5 doc sites | This is a real drift correction but it has been "deferred to v0.9" since v0.8 and is the kind of cleanup a single 5-minute git-grep should have caught any time it was raised. The kit's own process treated it as a tracked candidate (M-3, M-NEW-1) requiring evaluator-cycle handling. That is procedural overhead applied to a 5-character edit. |
| v0.7/v0.8 | Promotion sequencing of structural checks (Check A WARN→FAIL, Check B WARN→FAIL, Check C WARN→FAIL) | Each promotion was technically a defect (the WARN level meant the check was being run but ignored), but no real drift occurred in the WARN window — the promotions were retroactive ceremonies. |

### Ratio (qualitative)

Across v0.5–v0.9, roughly **40% of evaluator-caught items were real shipped-blocking defects, 35% were defensive closures of declared-but-unexercised rules, and 25% were process-internal corrections** (anchoring effects, ratio tracking, template adoption). The 40% is real value. The 35% is debatable insurance — Rule 16 / Rule 17 / pre-flight Check D-G genuinely harden the kit against the *next* contributor's drift, but their immediate user benefit is zero. The 25% is the part that has gone from cost-effective to overhead.

**The v0.5–v0.6 cycles were clearly net-positive.** The v0.7–v0.8 cycles were marginal-positive. The v0.9 cycle was, by the kit's own admission (`CHANGELOG.md:11`), an exercise in correcting the corrections (Protocol 1 reframed M-2 because the HANDOFF itself had drifted). When the evaluator corrects the evaluator, the process is approaching the limit of what protocol discipline can produce in isolation.

| # | Blocker | Severity | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|

**Schemas (pillars.json, milestones.json) are v1.0-ready** — additive-only across 9 versions, the breakage menu is enumerated, the migrations are documented.

**Bot hook API (`_gmkApiVersion: 1`) is v1.0-ready** — additive history is clean, no breaking changes shipped, the fallback paths are honest.

**SKILL contracts are v1.0-ready in spec** — preconditions are stable, refuse texts are concrete, Rule 14 cycle prevention is closed. What's untested is whether the contracts hold under real user load.

## §6 Recommended next move

**Recommendation: (b) v0.10 minor work, then v1.0 — but v0.10 must be different in kind from v0.5–v0.9.**

The v0.5–v0.9 cycles were process-internal: looking at the kit, finding gaps the kit creates for itself, closing them. v0.10 needs to be **dogfood-driven**, not process-driven.

Concretely:

1. **v0.10 = one real cycle on a real game**, even if small. The "GMK Dogfood" project the memory references (separate from ZooMerge) should run `/gmk-init` → at least one milestone reaching `/gmk-port` RE_PASS. Every defect that surfaces during that run becomes the v0.10 backlog. If zero defects surface — v1.0 ships with that as the headline evidence. If a flood of defects surface — Protocols 1/3/4 were measuring the wrong thing, and v1.0 was correctly blocked.
2. **Ship a QUICKSTART.md** alongside v0.10. It can be a transcript of #1.
3. **Resolve the `gmk-port` cap tension** — either split or formally allowlist with a Rule 2 extension to SKILLs. Don't ship v1.0 with the single largest doc violating the kit's own cap.
4. **Then bump to v1.0** — the schema/API/rules/contracts are already there.

Why not bump v1.0 now (option a): SemVer 1.0 is a public-API commitment. The kit's public API is the SKILL contracts + the schemas + the bot hook + the Rules. *Specifying* a contract isn't the same as having *verified* it survives contact with a user who didn't write it. The v0.5–v0.9 cycles demonstrate the kit's protocols work; they do not demonstrate the kit itself does. One real dogfood closes that gap — and is far cheaper than four more meta-evaluator cycles.

Why not "something else" (option c): the kit is not broken, it is not abandoned, and it does not need redesign. It needs a user. The right way to get there is the minimum path that produces external evidence.

**Predicted outcome of v0.10**: 2-5 real defects surface during dogfood (probably in the gmk-prototype/gmk-validate handoff, where the hook smoke check is most likely to find a user mistake the templates didn't model). Each one is a 30-minute fix. v1.0 ships 2 weeks later with 3 things v0.9 doesn't have: dogfood evidence, a quickstart, and the `gmk-port` cap reconciled.

## Closing note on the user's intuition check

The user offered two framings:

- (a) *"v0.9 is v1.0-worthy and the v0.5–v0.9 cycles were meta-perfectionism delaying release."*
- (b) *"v0.9 isn't v1.0-worthy and the cycles caught real things — there's another flow needed."*

My read: **(b), but with a sharp qualifier.** The "another flow" needed isn't more process — it's the missing axis the process can't measure: user evidence. The cycles caught real things in v0.5–v0.7 and progressively fewer real things in v0.8–v0.9. The intuition that *"if it can't reach 1.0 there's another flow"* is correct, and the missing flow is dogfood. Once a real user (even the author, but on a fresh non-meta project) crosses the kit's own gates, v1.0 ships with confidence. Until then, the kit is in the *"specified, internally consistent, externally untested"* state — a place where the difference between v0.9 and v1.0 is honesty, not capability.

The user's *original* intuition — *"보통 목표가 1.0에 끝 아니야?"* — is also correct as a heuristic: most v0.9s should bump to v1.0. This kit has been one realistic dogfood cycle away from doing exactly that for at least two releases.
